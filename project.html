Case study · 01

Alibaug Residence

Awas, Maharashtra
2024 — 2026

TypologyPrivate residence
Area420 m² on 0.8 ha
ScopeInterior architecture, joinery, landscape, art
StatusHandover, Nov 2026

A house that spends most of the year half outdoors.

The clients — a photographer and a doctor, with two teenagers and a great many visiting cousins — asked for “somewhere we can put forty people and still find a quiet chair.” The site is four hundred metres from the water, flat, and brutally hot from March to June.

So the plan gave up on the idea of a front door. Four low volumes sit around a shaded courtyard: sleeping to the north, cooking and eating to the east, a long verandah south, and a studio that floats slightly off the ground on the west edge. You cross the courtyard to get anywhere. In June the whole thing runs seven degrees cooler than the road outside.

Materially it is a short list, held to strictly. Kota stone underfoot throughout, honed rather than polished so it stays cool and shows water marks. Reclaimed Burma teak for every frame, door and shutter, from a single dismantled warehouse in Kochi. Walls in lime plaster mixed on site and burnished by hand, which means no two rooms are quite the same colour — something we argued for and the clients now point out to guests.

We removed two walls, added one, and the house finally had a middle.

Detail

One quarry, one lot

Every piece of stone in the house came from a single bed near Ramganj Mandi, ordered at once so the green stays consistent. The offcuts became the garden steps. Nothing left site.

Light

Measured in hours, not lux

We logged the sun across the site for a full year before drawing a single opening. Every window in the house is sized against a specific hour: the kitchen for six in the morning, the studio for four in the afternoon, the verandah for the ten minutes before the light goes.
